## Our comparison criteria

To compare sites that differ so much from one another, we focused on concrete criteria, not a subjective score:

- **Free without an account** — can you play right away, without creating a profile?
- **No paywall** — is the core of the game really accessible without a subscription?
- **Difficulty levels** — from beginner to expert, or just 2-3 tiers?
- **Daily challenge** — a grid shared by all players, with a ranking or not.
- **Printing** — the ability to generate a PDF to print.
- **Grid quality** — grids generated with a single, verified solution, solvable by pure logic (with no need to guess).

## The other good sudoku sites (in no particular order of preference)

### 2. Sudoku.com

The best-known and most-visited site in the category, by a wide margin. Sudoku.com covers all difficulty levels, offers daily challenges and detailed statistics, and works on every device. The flip side of that popularity: according to several independent comparisons, it is also one of the most ad-heavy sites, especially on mobile.

### 3. Web Sudoku (websudoku.com)

One of the pioneers of online sudoku, active since the early 2000s. Web Sudoku even supplies grids to newspapers and magazines. The interface is plain, deliberately simple, with the option to print grids — handy if you're after something minimalist without frills.

### 4. LiveSudoku

Online since 2006, LiveSudoku stands out for its **real-time multiplayer mode**, where you compete against other players on solving speed — a fairly rare feature elsewhere. An account is required, however, to access multiplayer and track your stats.

### 5. le-sudoku.fr

A long-standing French site, with grids at every level and pages dedicated to the rules and solving techniques. The interface looks more dated than that of newer sites, but the educational content around the game is solid.

### 6. franceinfo Jeux

The sudoku section of the franceinfo website publishes a new grid every day, free to access. Handy if you already read the news on franceinfo and want a sudoku break without switching tabs — though the offering is more limited than on a specialized site (fewer levels, no ranked challenge).

### 7. NYT Games (New York Times)

The New York Times app offers a daily sudoku at three levels, with a very polished interface. The major difference from the previous sites: it is a **paid** service, bundled into the NYT Games subscription — a good choice if you already subscribe for the crossword or Connections, less relevant if you only want free sudoku.
