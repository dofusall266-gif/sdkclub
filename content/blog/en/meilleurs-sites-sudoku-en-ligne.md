## Our criteria, in detail

- **No account** — can you play right away, with no profile to create?

## The other good sudoku sites (in no particular order)

### 2. Sudoku.com

**In brief**: the heavyweight of the space, feature-complete, but ad-heavy on mobile.

By far the best-known and most-visited site in the space. Sudoku.com covers several difficulty levels, offers daily challenges and detailed stats, and works on every device. The flip side of that popularity: according to several independent comparisons, it's also one of the most ad-heavy sites out there, particularly on mobile.

### 3. Web Sudoku (websudoku.com)

**In brief**: simple, long-established, no frills — but no ranked daily challenge.

One of the pioneers of online sudoku, active since the early 2000s. Web Sudoku actually supplies puzzles to newspapers and magazines. The interface is plain and deliberately simple, with the option to print puzzles — handy if you're after something minimalist.

### 4. LiveSudoku

**In brief**: the only site on this list with real-time multiplayer — at the cost of an account.

Online since 2006, LiveSudoku stands out for its **real-time multiplayer mode**, where you race other players on solving speed — a fairly rare feature elsewhere. An account is required, however, to access multiplayer and track your stats.

### 5. le-sudoku.fr

**In brief**: a long-established French site, with genuinely useful educational content around the game.

A French site with puzzles across several levels and dedicated pages on rules and solving techniques. The interface feels more dated than newer sites, but the educational content around the game is solid.

### 6. franceinfo Jeux

**In brief**: convenient if you already read franceinfo, limited if you're after more.

The sudoku section of the French news outlet franceinfo publishes a new puzzle every day, free to access. Convenient if you already read the news there and want a sudoku break without switching tabs — though the offering stays more limited than a dedicated site (no choice of level, no leaderboard).

### 7. NYT Games (New York Times)

**In brief**: the most polished visually — but paid, through the NYT Games subscription.

The New York Times app offers a daily sudoku at three levels, with a very polished interface. The major difference from the sites above: it's a **paid** service, bundled into the NYT Games subscription — a good choice if you're already subscribed for the Crossword or Connections, less relevant if you're only after free sudoku.
