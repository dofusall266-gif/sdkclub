**In short**: a sudoku grid is solved by deduction, never by guessing. Techniques range from the simplest (singles) to the most advanced (X-Wing, Y-Wing, Swordfish). Mastering the first ones is enough for Easy and Medium; the next become useful from Hard and essential in Expert.

This guide ranks techniques **in the order you learn them**. For each step you will find something to understand and something to practise. One golden rule: before moving on to an advanced technique, make sure you have exhausted the previous ones.

## Level 1: singles

This is the basis of every solve.
- **Naked single**: a cell has only one possible candidate left.
- **Hidden single**: in a row, column or box, a digit has only one possible spot.

These two techniques are enough for Easy grids. You can practise them directly by [playing a grid](/).

## Level 2: alignments

When, within a box, a digit is only possible on a single row (or column), you can remove it from the rest of that row: that is **pointing**. Conversely, when in a row a digit is only possible in a single box, you remove it from the rest of the box: that is **claiming**. From here on, **notes become essential**.

## Level 3: pairs and triples

Two cells in the same unit holding only the same two candidates form a **pair**: those two digits cannot be elsewhere in the unit. The principle extends to **triples**. The “hidden” version (the digits are hidden among other candidates) is harder to see.

## Level 4: advanced techniques

This is the level of Expert grids. Each technique now has its own **illustrated lesson with exercises**:

- **[X-Wing](/techniques/x-wing)**: a four-cell rectangle on one digit that lets you eliminate that digit in two columns (also read [the X-Wing article](/blog/technique-x-wing)).
- **[Y-Wing](/techniques/y-wing)**: a pivot and two two-candidate wings (also read [the Y-Wing article](/blog/technique-y-wing)).
- **[Swordfish](/techniques/swordfish)**: the X-Wing's big brother, on three rows and three columns (also read [the Swordfish article](/blog/technique-swordfish)).

## Beyond: chains

Some very hard grids need longer reasoning: chains of two-candidate cells or strong links between spots. They extend the ideas of the Y-Wing and the X-Wing, but are best learned once those are mastered.

## How to improve

1. **Play one level below** the one that blocks you, and note what you are missing.
2. **Use notes** from Medium level.
3. **Practise on real situations** with the [exercises on the Techniques page](/techniques).
4. **Move up a level** when you finish without a mistake.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do you need every technique to finish an Expert sudoku?**
Not all, but generally at least a few advanced ones: an Expert grid is often unlocked with a Y-Wing or an X-Wing.

**Can you solve a sudoku by guessing?**
It is possible but it is not really sudoku: every Sudoku-Club grid is checked to be solvable by pure deduction.

**Where should a beginner start?**
With singles, on Easy grids. The [sudoku rules](/regles) explain everything in a few minutes.
